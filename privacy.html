Privacy policy

Publisher: Oytun Yegin
Effective: October 9, 2026

This policy applies to Toybox Turret's private TestFlight test-ad builds: version 1.0.1 (68) and version 1.0.2 (70, 71, and 72). These are single-player builds with no account, purchases, or global leaderboard. None of these builds automatically uploads gameplay progress or publisher diagnostics. Build 67 is ad-free. The private TestFlight test-ad builds include optional Google test rewarded ads; these do not earn advertising revenue for the publisher.

Progress on your device

The game saves your buddy name, achievements, outfits, world unlocks, personal scores, lessons, weapon discoveries, and settings. It records interrupted-run progress so it can be counted once after relaunch. There is no cloud save.

Local game-quality information and optional export in 1.0.2 (70, 71, and 72)

All three private version 1.0.2 builds (70, 71, and 72) keep the same limited game-quality summary on your device: fixed daily count categories for up to 31 UTC days and up to 200 recent event labels. This summary does not include your buddy name, scores, precise location, advertising identifiers, raw exceptions, or stack paths.

All three private version 1.0.2 builds (70, 71, and 72) have no configured publisher diagnostics endpoint, do not create a diagnostics upload ID, and do not automatically upload game-quality counts. In any of these builds, you can tap “Share game diagnostics” in About & privacy to send the local summary through your device's share sheet. The export omits the endpoint and installation ID and goes only to a recipient you choose.

Optional rewarded ads in the TestFlight beta

You can choose to watch a Google test ad to activate XP auto-collect for the current run. Level choices remain manual. Ads are loaded only after you choose to watch; Google consent information may be refreshed separately. The beta requests non-personalized ads and uses Google's official demo advertising identifiers, not a live publisher placement.

The native Google Mobile Ads and User Messaging Platform SDKs may process your IP address and approximate location, device or app identifiers, advertising interactions, crash information, and performance diagnostics. Test ads and non-personalized requests do not establish that no data is collected. Google handles this data under its own policies for advertising delivery, reporting, fraud prevention, diagnostics, and analytics.

When applicable, Google's consent form controls whether an ad can be requested. Ad privacy choices can be reopened in Settings when Google's SDK requires that option. Declining or skipping an ad does not prevent normal gameplay. The game does not ask for precise location, contacts, microphone access, or cross-app tracking permission.

See Google's iOS SDK data disclosure and Google's privacy policy. These disclosures cover the native ads-enabled TestFlight builds described above; build 67 does not contain the advertising SDK.

Sharing is optional

A share card can include your buddy name, outfit, world, score, boss wins, weapon build, and a game or Daily Toybox link. You choose where it goes in your phone's share sheet. In all three private version 1.0.2 builds (70, 71, and 72), choosing Save Image can cause iOS to ask permission to save the run card to your photo library; generic sharing does not select or read images from your photo library. The game attempts to remove its temporary image afterward; your phone or a recipient may keep a copy.

Your choices

Change sound, music, vibration, and reduced motion in Settings. Deleting the app may remove local progress. There is no in-app reset or cloud restore. Removing the app does not erase an image you already shared.

Website and support

These pages are hosted by GitHub Pages. The page code adds no analytics, tracking pixels, cookies, or contact forms. GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security.

Support issues are public. Your GitHub profile, message, and attachments are visible to the publisher and other visitors. The publisher uses what you choose to provide to respond and investigate problems.

See GitHub's privacy statement for its handling of website, account, and support data.

Privacy questions

Contact the publisher

Ask a general privacy question or make a request about a support issue. Include only information you want to share publicly. GitHub handles requests about GitHub account and service data.
